    How to play

    You start with a five-letter word. Each round you drop some of its letters down into the next word and add one more, making a word one letter shorter, until you are down to a single letter.

    Dropping is not discarding. The letters you drop are the letters you take with you. Everything you leave behind is gone. The board shows it: letters that dropped turn grey with an arrow under them, and the ones left behind fade away.

    1. 5 → 4 Drop three letters in a row, then add one.
    2. 4 → 3 Drop two letters in a row, then add one.
    3. 3 → 2 Drop one letter, then add one.
    4. 2 → 1 If your two-letter word holds an A or an I, it falls on its own. Those are the only one-letter words.

    Two rules

    • The letters you drop land side by side and in the same order, so the letter you add goes on one end, never in the middle.
    • Every letter you add must be one you have not used yet. Anything already on the board, the opening word included, is greyed out on the keyboard.

    Taking a turn

    Tap the letters you want to take down. They drop as soon as you have enough of them. Type a letter, tap the empty slot at either end (or use the arrow keys) to choose which end it lands on, then tap the green check. Words come from a Scrabble dictionary, so a few odd ones are legal.

    Scoring

    • 2 points for every word you make.
    • 1, 2 or 3 points for the letter it brings in, depending on how awkward that letter is to use. The small number in the corner of each key says which, the way a Scrabble tile does, and the letter carries it onto the board once you play it. The last letter falls without adding one, so that round is worth 2.
    • 3 points for getting all the way down to a single letter. Stop short and you lose them, so the expensive letter is not always worth taking.
    • Undo costs 1 point once there is a word to take back. Backing out of a split you have not committed is free, and your score never goes below zero.

    Every deal has its own best possible score, shown beside your running total from the start. Reaching it is a balance: the awkward letters pay more, but taking one without a route to the bottom costs you the finish, and a bad split can leave you with nothing to make at all. When a game ends you can reveal a run that scored the lot, to see what you missed. You can also give up part way through, which keeps the points you have and shows you the rest. Either way, looking closes that game: Undo stops working, so being stuck is a real choice between paying for another go and giving up to see how it was done.

    The one thing to plan for

    Nothing falls unless your two-letter word holds an A or an I, so work out early where that letter is coming from. A and I obey the same rule as every other letter: once one is on the board it can only be carried down, never added again. If your opening word already holds an A or an I, choose the splits that bring it all the way with you. If it holds neither, keep one of them unspent for the final round.

    Hints

    Turn Hints on and every letter that makes a word from where you stand gets a green ring. It is there to learn with, so a run played with hints on counts as practice and cannot be shared.
